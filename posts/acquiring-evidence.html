The Art of Evidence Acquisition

Week 4 Session

The gold rule: capture volatile evidence first. Using tools like tcpdump and Wireshark, we practice collecting accurate, reliable, and court-admissible artifacts while maintaining a strict chain of custody.

Key Principles

Order of Volatility: Capture the most volatile evidence first (RAM, network connections) before less volatile data (disk logs).

Chain of Custody: Maintain detailed documentation of who handled evidence, when, and how.

Integrity: Always use hashing (MD5, SHA-256) to ensure evidence hasn't been tampered with.

Order of Volatility

Network evidence follows a strict order of volatility. The most volatile data must be captured first because it disappears quickly:

  1. Registers and Cache: Most volatile, lost immediately
  2. RAM: Lost when system powers down
  3. Network Connections: Active sessions that end when connections close
  4. Routing Tables: Can change frequently
  5. ARP Cache: MAC-to-IP mappings
  6. Process Table: Running processes
  7. Kernel Statistics: System counters
  8. Log Files: More persistent but can be overwritten
  9. Disk: Most persistent, but still can be modified

Tools for Evidence Acquisition

tcpdump

Command-line packet analyzer that allows capturing network traffic. Essential for capturing packets in real-time and saving them to PCAP files for later analysis.

Wireshark

Graphical network protocol analyzer that provides deep inspection of hundreds of protocols. Excellent for both live capture and offline analysis of captured packets.

Chain of Custody

Maintaining a proper chain of custody is critical for legal admissibility. Every piece of evidence must be:

  • Documented with timestamps
  • Hashed to verify integrity
  • Stored securely
  • Tracked through every transfer

Hashing for Integrity

Always create cryptographic hashes (MD5, SHA-256) of evidence immediately after capture. This provides a digital fingerprint that can verify the evidence hasn't been altered. Store hashes separately from the evidence itself.
