Foundations of the Digital Hunt

Week 1 Session

Network forensics is more than just looking at logs; it's about tracking hackers through cyberspace. This week, we explored the OSCAR methodology—Obtain info, Strategize, Collect, Analyze, and Report—to ensure our investigations are legally defensible and factual.

Key Concepts

Definition: Monitoring and analyzing network traffic to find legal evidence, detect intrusions, or gather information.

Methodologies: Uses OSCAR (Obtain Info, Strategize, Collect, Analyze, Report) and TAARA (Trigger, Acquire, Analysis, Report, Action).

Key Challenge: Unlike computer forensics, network data is highly volatile and often exists only in RAM.

Understanding Network Forensics

Network forensics involves the capture, recording, and analysis of network events in order to discover the source of security attacks or other problem incidents. Unlike traditional computer forensics where data is stored on disk, network forensics deals with data that is transient and volatile.

The OSCAR Methodology

  • Obtain Info: Gather initial information about the incident
  • Strategize: Plan your investigation approach
  • Collect: Capture network evidence following proper procedures
  • Analyze: Examine the collected data for patterns and anomalies
  • Report: Document findings in a legally defensible manner
