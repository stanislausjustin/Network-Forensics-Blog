Guest Lecture: The Next Industrial Revolution

Week 11 Session

This week we had the privilege of attending a special guest lecture session organized by Computer Science Binus International. The lecture explored "The Next Industrial Revolution: Harnessing Agentic and Physical AIs" and provided fascinating insights into how artificial intelligence is reshaping industries and what this means for cybersecurity and network forensics.

Event Details

Title: The Next Industrial Revolution: Harnessing Agentic and Physical AIs

Speaker: Professor Ki-Yong Oh, School of Mechanical Engineering, Hanyang University

Date: Friday, December 19, 2025

Time: 13.30 – 15.00 WIB

Location: Binus International Auditorium, Binus FX Campus (6th Floor)

Understanding Agentic and Physical AIs

Professor Oh's lecture delved into two emerging categories of artificial intelligence that are driving the next industrial revolution:

Agentic AI

Agentic AI refers to AI systems that can act autonomously, make decisions, and take actions to achieve specific goals. These systems can:

  • Operate independently without constant human intervention
  • Learn from their environment and adapt their behavior
  • Make complex decisions based on multiple data sources
  • Coordinate with other AI systems and human operators

Physical AI

Physical AI involves AI systems that interact with the physical world through robotics, IoT devices, and embedded systems. These systems:

  • Bridge the gap between digital intelligence and physical actions
  • Control physical processes and machinery
  • Interact with the environment through sensors and actuators
  • Enable automation in manufacturing, logistics, and infrastructure

Implications for Network Forensics

As a network forensics student, this lecture raised important questions about how these AI technologies will impact our field:

New Attack Vectors

The proliferation of AI-powered systems creates new attack surfaces:

  • AI Model Manipulation: Attackers may target AI training data or models to cause malicious behavior
  • Autonomous System Compromise: Agentic AIs could be hijacked to perform unauthorized actions
  • Physical-World Attacks: Compromised physical AIs could cause real-world damage
  • AI-Generated Attacks: Attackers might use AI to generate sophisticated, adaptive malware

Forensic Challenges

Investigating incidents involving AI systems presents unique challenges:

  • Decision Traceability: Understanding why an AI system made specific decisions can be difficult
  • Distributed Systems: Agentic AIs may operate across multiple networks and systems
  • Continuous Learning: AI systems that learn continuously may change behavior over time, complicating investigations
  • Data Volume: AI systems generate massive amounts of data that must be analyzed

AI as a Forensic Tool

On the positive side, AI technologies also offer powerful tools for network forensics:

  • Automated Pattern Recognition: AI can identify anomalies and patterns in network traffic faster than humans
  • Threat Detection: Machine learning models can detect previously unknown attack patterns
  • Log Analysis: AI can process and correlate massive log files efficiently
  • Predictive Analysis: AI can predict potential security incidents before they occur

Key Takeaways

The lecture emphasized that we are entering an era where AI systems will be deeply integrated into industrial processes, critical infrastructure, and everyday systems. For network forensics professionals, this means:

  • We must understand how AI systems work to effectively investigate incidents involving them
  • New forensic methodologies will be needed to trace AI decision-making processes
  • AI tools will become essential for managing the increasing complexity and volume of network data
  • Security and forensics must be built into AI systems from the ground up

Looking Forward

As the industrial revolution driven by AI continues to unfold, network forensics will need to evolve. We'll need to develop new skills in:

  • AI system architecture and operation
  • Machine learning model analysis
  • IoT and embedded system forensics
  • AI-powered forensic tools and techniques

This guest lecture was a valuable reminder that network forensics doesn't exist in isolation—it must adapt to the technologies it investigates. As AI becomes more prevalent, our field must evolve to meet new challenges and leverage new opportunities.

About the Speaker

Professor Ki-Yong Oh is a distinguished professor in the School of Mechanical Engineering at Hanyang University, one of South Korea's leading technical universities. His expertise in mechanical engineering and AI systems provides unique insights into how physical and digital worlds are converging through artificial intelligence.
