Weekly Learning Sessions

Week 1

Foundations of the Digital Hunt

Network forensics is more than just looking at logs; it's about tracking hackers through cyberspace. This week, we explored the OSCAR methodology—Obtain info, Strategize, Collect, Analyze, and Report—to ensure our investigations are legally defensible and factual.

Week 2

Where the Evidence Hides

Evidence is the foundation of any investigation. We compared heavy-duty Packet Captures (PCAPs) with lightweight Flow Records like NetFlow. Real-world cases like the Equifax and Tokopedia breaches show how vital firewall and database logs are in confirming data exfiltration.

Week 3

Decoding the Protocol Trail

Every network communication leaves a fingerprint. We looked at how transport layers (TCP/UDP) and application layers (DNS, HTTP) provide clues. For example, abnormal TCP flags can indicate an attacker trying to cover their tracks.

Week 4

The Art of Evidence Acquisition

The gold rule: capture volatile evidence first. Using tools like tcpdump and Wireshark, we practice collecting accurate, reliable, and court-admissible artifacts while maintaining a strict chain of custody.

Week 5

Deep Dive into Traffic Patterns

Traffic analysis focuses on communication patterns—who, when, and how much—rather than just the content.

Week 6

The Traffic Analyst's Toolkit

This week was all about looking past the "what" and into the "how" of network communication. Traffic analysis allows us to identify scanning or exfiltration even when encryption hides the payload.

Read More PDF
Week 7

Clearing the Air: Wireless Forensics

Wireless forensics brings unique challenges like signal range and encryption. We learned to use "Monitor Mode" to capture 802.11 management frames, which helps in identifying rogue Access Points and deauthentication attacks.

Week 8

Following the Intruder's Footprints

In forensics, we track actions, not names. By using IDS logs, we can identify unusual port usage or protocol misuse like DNS tunneling. Remember: blocking an attack with IPS is good for security, but it often makes the forensic evidence weaker.

Week 9

Log Correlation

In log correlation we need to make sure that timestamps is correct and the integrity of the logs is not changed. The most important activity is the centralization and normalization of all logs into a standardized format with corrected timestamps.

Week 10

Infrastructure Devices

Infrastructure devices like switches and routers are gold mines for evidence. We focused on CAM and ARP tables to map physical locations to IP addresses. Always remember to check if the router itself has been compromised!
